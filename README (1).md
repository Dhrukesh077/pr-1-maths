## 📌 Project Overview

The institute is launching a new **"Expectation Decider"** model that predicts whether a student will pass a competitive mathematics exam based on historical academic behavior. As a junior data analyst, this project analyzes a sample dataset of **200 students** — covering study hours, attendance, group discussion participation, and previous test performance — to uncover probability patterns and derive meaningful, data-backed insights.

The full analysis applies core probability and statistics concepts: basic probability theory, empirical vs. theoretical probability, random variables and distributions, Venn diagrams, contingency tables, conditional/joint/marginal probability, independence testing, and Bayes' Theorem.

## 🎯 Project Objective

> Analyze the probability patterns from the dataset and derive meaningful insights into what most influences a student's likelihood of passing the competitive exam.

## 🔬 Methodology — Task-by-Task Breakdown

### 1. Understanding the Basics
Defined probability, walked through key terminology (sample space, event, random variable, independent/dependent/mutually exclusive events, joint/marginal/conditional probability), and identified three concrete probability events from the dataset (high attendance, group discussion participation, exam pass).

### 2. Types of Events
Distinguished **empirical probability** (P(Pass) = 150/200 = 0.75, calculated directly from observed data) from **theoretical probability** (P(Heads) = 0.5 on a fair coin, derived from logical reasoning about equally likely outcomes).

### 3. Random Variable & Probability Distribution
Modeled **X = number of students passing out of 3 randomly selected** as a **Binomial(n=3, p=0.75)** random variable, built its full probability distribution table, and computed:
- **Mean:** E(X) = n·p = 2.25
- **Variance:** Var(X) = n·p·(1-p) = 0.5625

📊 **Chart — Probability Distribution of X:**

![Binomial Probability Distribution](ss/1.png)

The distribution is left-skewed, with the highest probabilities concentrated at X=2 and X=3 — consistent with the high overall pass rate (75%) in the dataset.

### 4. Venn Diagram in Probability
Compared two events — **Event A:** study_hours > 10 hrs/week, and **Event B:** attendance > 80% — and computed the overlap.

📊 **Chart — Venn Diagram:**

![Venn Diagram of Study Hours vs Attendance](ss/2.png)

| Region | Count |
|---|---|
| Only A (high study hours) | 70 |
| Only B (high attendance) | 39 |
| A ∩ B (both) | 50 |
| Neither | 41 |

### 5. Contingency Table & Probability Calculations
Built a contingency table of `group_discussion` vs `final_exam_pass` and calculated:
- **Joint probability** P(Group Discussion = Yes AND Pass) = 95/200 = **0.475**
- **Marginal probability** P(Pass) = 150/200 = **0.75**
- **Conditional probability** P(Pass | Group Discussion = Yes) = 95/125 = **0.76**

📊 **Chart — Group Discussion vs Exam Result:**

![Group Discussion vs Exam Result](ss/3.png)

### 6. Understanding Relationships
Tested whether group discussion participation and passing are **independent, dependent, or mutually exclusive**:
- P(GD=Yes) × P(Pass) = 0.625 × 0.75 = 0.469 (expected if independent)
- Actual observed joint probability = 0.475 (very close)
- **Conclusion:** The two events are approximately **independent** and are **not mutually exclusive** (many students both participate and pass).

### 7. Bayes' Theorem Application
Given historical rates — P(High Attendance | Pass) = 0.70, P(High Attendance | Fail) = 0.40, P(High Attendance) = 0.60 — applied Bayes' Theorem to find:

P(Pass | High Attendance) = [P(High Attendance | Pass) × P(Pass)] / P(High Attendance)
= (0.70 × 0.75) / 0.60 = **0.875**

**Result: 87.5%** — meaningfully higher than the 75% base rate, confirming attendance as a strong positive predictor of passing.

## 💡 Key Insights

| Factor | Finding | Strength of Influence |
|---|---|---|
| **Attendance** | P(Pass \| High Attendance) = 87.5% vs. 75% base rate | 🔴 Strong positive predictor |
| **Study Hours** | 50 students overlap in high study hours + high attendance — the strongest-performing segment | 🟠 Moderate, compounding factor |
| **Group Discussion** | P(Pass \| GD=Yes) = 76% ≈ P(Pass) = 75% — near independence | 🟡 Weak/negligible standalone influence |
| **Previous Test Score** | Not modeled with a formal probability calculation here — recommended for further analysis (e.g. correlation, logistic regression) | ⚪ Unexplored in this phase |

**Bottom line:** Attendance is the single strongest lever for predicting exam success, and students who combine strong attendance with high weekly study hours form the highest-probability-of-passing group. Group discussion participation alone shows little statistical association with passing in this sample.
